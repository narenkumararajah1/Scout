# Scout Reports Design Specification

## Purpose

The Reports module is responsible for transforming Scout's intelligence into polished, executive-ready deliverables.

Reports should communicate business insights rather than simply displaying collected data.

Every report generated by Scout should be suitable for:

- Executive meetings
- Customer engagements
- Internal strategy discussions
- Sales planning
- Opportunity reviews
- Leadership presentations

Reports should require little to no manual editing before use.

---

# Design Objectives

Reports shall:

- Tell a complete business story.
- Present information in a logical sequence.
- Prioritize executive summaries over raw data.
- Use visualizations to improve understanding.
- Maintain a professional appearance.
- Be easily exportable.
- Build confidence in Scout's AI analysis.

Reports should feel as though they were prepared by an experienced business consultant.

---

# Report Philosophy

Scout reports are not data exports.

They are business intelligence documents.

Every section should answer an important business question.

Users should finish reading a report with a clear understanding of:

- What happened
- Why it matters
- What opportunities exist
- What actions should be taken

# Cover Page

The cover page should contain:

Report Title

Company Name

Generated Date

Prepared By Scout

Report Type

Confidentiality Indicator (Optional)

Company Logo

The design should remain minimal and professional.

---

# Executive Summary

Every report begins with an executive summary.

The summary should answer:

What happened?

Why does it matter?

What should we do?

Length:

Two to four concise paragraphs.

The executive summary is the most important section of every report.

---

# Key Insights

Summarize the most important discoveries.

Examples:

- Company accelerating AI investments.
- Cloud modernization initiative identified.
- Executive leadership recently changed.
- Hiring indicates platform expansion.
- Strong opportunity for digital transformation.

Limit to five to ten insights.

---

# Business Analysis

Provide structured business intelligence.

Examples:

Business Model

Industry

Markets

Growth Strategy

Strategic Priorities

Recent Developments

Avoid excessive background information.

---

# Opportunity Analysis

Explain every identified opportunity.

Each opportunity should include:

Opportunity

Reasoning

Supporting Evidence

Business Impact

Confidence

Recommended Services

Priority

Suggested Next Step

Users should understand exactly why the recommendation exists.

---

# Capability Alignment

Map opportunities directly to Innominds capabilities.

Each recommendation should explain:

Business Challenge

Recommended Capability

Expected Value

Why It Fits

Potential Engagement

This section should clearly demonstrate business relevance.

---

# Visualizations

Reports should include meaningful visualizations.

Examples:

Opportunity Score

Technology Adoption

Hiring Trends

Timeline

Executive Changes

Industry Comparison

Capability Mapping

Charts should complement—not replace—the written narrative.

---

# AI Transparency

Every AI-generated insight should identify:

Reasoning

Supporting Evidence

Confidence

Generation Timestamp

Scout should remain transparent about AI-generated content.

---

# Recommendations

Recommendations should be practical and actionable.

Examples:

Schedule executive meeting.

Generate outreach email.

Prepare technical workshop.

Monitor hiring activity.

Compare competitors.

Develop proposal.

Recommendations should be prioritized.

---

# Next Steps

Every report concludes with recommended actions.

Suggested structure:

Immediate

Short-Term

Long-Term

Each recommendation should be clear and achievable.

# Success Criteria

A successful report enables readers to answer the following questions without opening Scout:

- What happened?
- Why does it matter?
- What opportunities exist?
- What evidence supports those opportunities?
- Which services should we recommend?
- What actions should we take next?

If a report cannot stand alone as a business document, it should be redesigned.

---

# Final Principle

Scout reports should not feel like exported application screens.

They should feel like professionally prepared executive consulting deliverables that clearly communicate insights, recommendations, and next steps while reinforcing confidence in Scout's intelligence.
